    What makes a road fun?

    Every paved road in OpenStreetMap is measured every 10 metres. A road is scored on its best stretch (up to 15 km), so a great pass isn't dragged down by the valley road leading to it. That stretch scores:

    1. Bends. How tight the road turns at each point: sweeping (radius under 175 m), flowing (100 m), tight (60 m) or hairpin-tight (30 m). Every kilometre in a bend counts, weighted by your taste: towards Sweepers fast, flowing roads rise; towards Hairpins switchback stacks win. Bends through villages count only 30%: nice to look at, not to ride hard.
    2. × Terrain. Up to ×2 for 1,200 m of climb, or up to ×1.6 for rolling crests and dips, so hilly roads without a big climb aren't punished.
    3. × Scenery. Up to ×1.5 where the view opens up below the road (balcony roads above lakes and valleys), runs along a steep drop-off, or passes a marked viewpoint.
    4. × Hairpins. The signature of a real pass (150° or more of turning within 120 m): up to ×1.5, more or less with your taste.

    × Good km. Finally, how much of the road is good riding: the kilometres at least half as good as its best stretch, up to 30 km. Meh stretches neither help nor hurt. A 6 km gem counts ×0.63, 15 km ×1, 30 km of greatness ×1.41.

    On the map, the hot spots glow orange and ★ marks the single best bit.

    The scale is fixed, so scores compare anywhere, and your taste reshuffles roads without inflating them. 100 would be a perfect road: scores up to 80 are linear, above that they're squeezed so nothing quite gets there. The best road around Geneva scores in the low 90s.

    Not in the score, but shown as warnings: narrow or single-lane stretches, tolls, tunnels, and seasonal closures. Unpaved, private and farm-only roads are left out entirely.

    Road data changes: check closures and conditions before you ride.
