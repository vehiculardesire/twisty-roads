    What makes a road fun?

    Every paved road in OpenStreetMap is measured every 10 metres. A road is scored on its best stretch (up to 15 km), so a great pass isn't dragged down by the valley road leading to it. Its bends are the base, and terrain, scenery and hairpins each add a bonus on top (they don't multiply, so a road isn't rewarded just for having a bit of everything):

    1. Bends. How tight the road turns at each point: sweeping (radius under 175 m), flowing (100 m), tight (60 m) or hairpin-tight (30 m). Every kilometre in a bend counts, weighted by your taste: towards Sweepers fast, flowing roads rise; towards Hairpins switchback stacks win. Bends through villages count only 30%: nice to look at, not to ride hard.
    2. + Terrain. Up to +0.5 for 1,500 m of climb, or up to +0.3 for rolling crests and dips. Kept modest on purpose: a low road that's all bends (like the Tail of the Dragon) is still a great road.
    3. + Scenery. Up to +0.5 for high alpine terrain above the treeline, or where the view opens up below the road (balcony roads above lakes and valleys), runs along a steep drop-off, or passes a marked viewpoint.
    4. + Hairpins. The signature of a real pass (150° or more of turning within 120 m): +0.5 for 20, and still a bit more for Stelvio-style stacks of 40 or 60. More or less with your taste.

    × Good km. Finally, how much of the road is good riding: the kilometres at least half as good as its best stretch, up to 30 km. Meh stretches neither help nor hurt. A 6 km gem counts ×0.63, 15 km ×1, 30 km of greatness ×1.41.

    On the map, the hot spots glow orange and ★ marks the single best bit.

    The scale

    Fixed, so scores compare anywhere, and your taste reshuffles roads without inflating them. It's calibrated on the world's benchmark roads: Stelvio 95, Grossglockner 81, Transfăgărășan 81, Tail of the Dragon 71. 100 is the ceiling; a road that measures even higher still shows 100.

    What it rewards, and what it can't see

    It's a measurement, not a verdict. It rewards lots of bends packed close together, hairpins, climbing and open views. Roads that concentrate all of that score very high even if they're not famous: Col de L'Arpettaz near Albertville hits the ceiling with 57 hairpins in 26 km, more bends per kilometre than the Stelvio, although part of it is a narrow lane.

    What the data can't see: how wide the road is (it's rarely mapped), surface quality, traffic, waterfalls, glaciers and cliff walls, how famous or atmospheric a road is, and whether it's open today. So a high score means "this will be very twisty", and a tight mountain lane and a wide famous pass can score the same. Read the warnings and the breakdown on each road, and trust your own taste.

    Not in the score, but shown as warnings: narrow or single-lane stretches, tolls, tunnels, and seasonal closures. Unpaved, private and farm-only roads are left out entirely.
